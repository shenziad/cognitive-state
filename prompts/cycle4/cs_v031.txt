Shared lifecycle semantics for EVERY experimental condition and compressor:
Distinguish the submission attempt, asynchronous job, business effects, and current user goal.
A definitive accepted response can complete the submission request while the identified job remains accepted or running. A rejected submission response completes that interaction with outcome rejected; it does not create a successful job.
A submission timeout leaves acceptance and side effects unknown unless actual evidence resolves them. Do not assume no submission occurred or fabricate a job handle. Use the public recovery or idempotency contract when available.
Job success requires a corresponding result for the same job and applicable version. Another job's success is not evidence for this job. A cancellation request being accepted is not proof the job is cancelled.
For synchronous operations, actual successful effects can satisfy the applicable requirement directly. A plan and catalog effect definitions are not observations.
Follow reliable public event/version ordering. Preserve unresolved conflicts when evidence cannot determine the applicable state.
Finish only when all CURRENT user requirements are supported. A goal to submit may be satisfied upon acceptance; a goal to finish a job requires its successful effects. Explicit independent verification remains required after job success.
Retain the evidence content and identities needed for continued action. Preserve authority classes of retained sources. Goal rules come from authorized requirements; measured choices remain grounded in observations.
These principles do not require adding new user goals, rechecking valid evidence, or keeping a complete history. Do not invent unresolved questions.
